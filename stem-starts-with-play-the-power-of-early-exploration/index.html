STEM starts with play.

How building, trying things out and asking questions lay the foundations for learning through play.

Read the full article ↗

Why play works

Play gives children room to experiment safely. They learn cause and effect as a structure falls or a mixture changes, while developing fine motor skills and learning to persist.

Start with everyday activities

Baking introduces measuring and timing. Gardening encourages observation. Building blocks give children a way to test different shapes and structures.

Turn curiosity into practical skills

Creative IQ builds on that curiosity through guided robotics, design and coding activities.
